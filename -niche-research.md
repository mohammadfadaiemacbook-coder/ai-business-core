# AI Business Core: YouTube Niche Research System

**Status:** Research framework — live market research has not yet been completed.

**Goal:** Select a viable niche for a faceless English-language YouTube channel that a beginner can produce consistently with a very limited budget.

**Decision rule:** No niche will be selected based on assumptions alone. The final decision must be supported by recorded evidence and a small production test.

---

## 0. Ground Rules

- **Verified fact:** Information directly observed from a source and recorded with its URL, access date, and relevant observation.
- **Hypothesis:** A plausible but untested assumption. Label it clearly as `Hypothesis`.
- **Tool estimate:** A metric estimated by a third-party tool. Record the tool name, date, and limitations.
- Never invent search volumes, revenue, RPM, audience size, or performance statistics.
- Public view counts are observations, not proof of search demand or future performance.
- A successful competitor does not guarantee that a new channel will succeed.
- Prefer free tools, original explanations, practical demonstrations, and repeatable production workflows.
- Verify current prices, free-tier restrictions, licenses, and YouTube policies before relying on them.
- Do not create accounts, spend money, publish content, or take irreversible actions during the research phase.
- Require the owner's explicit approval before publishing, spending money, creating accounts, or performing sensitive or irreversible actions.
- Never store passwords, API keys, recovery codes, or private credentials in this repository.
- Do not choose the final niche until the evidence review is complete.
- If evidence is insufficient, report `Insufficient evidence` instead of forcing a decision.

## 1. Research Plan

### Phase A: Screen the Candidate Niches — Day 1

Start with the 12 candidate niches in Section 3.

For each niche, write a one-sentence channel promise:

"I help [specific audience] achieve [specific outcome] using [content format or approach]."

Then identify three beginner-friendly video topics that could test the niche.

Do not conduct extensive research on all 12 niches yet. First eliminate options that clearly require expensive production, specialist credentials, inaccessible resources, or excessive reliance on copyrighted material.

### Phase B: Run a Small Demand Check — Days 2–3

For each of the 12 niches:

1. Select three specific beginner-oriented search phrases.
2. Search each phrase on YouTube.
3. Record the five most relevant organic results, including video title, URL, view count, publication date, and channel name.
4. Record relevant YouTube autocomplete suggestions.
5. Check up to three representative search terms in Google Trends over five years. Record the region, date range, search type, and whether interest appears to be rising, stable, falling, or unclear.
6. Find recurring audience questions in relevant Reddit communities, forums, or public discussions.
7. Record contradictory evidence and missing information.

Use the same collection method for every candidate.

YouTube search rankings are personalized and change over time. Record the date and avoid treating one search result page as a definitive market measurement.

Google Trends shows relative search interest, not absolute search volume. A low or missing Trends score does not by itself prove that a niche has no audience.

### Phase C: Research the Four Strongest Candidates — Days 3–4

Choose up to four candidates based on preliminary evidence and production feasibility.

For each shortlisted niche:

#### 1. Demand

- Expand the search sample to at least five relevant search phrases.
- Record up to five relevant results per phrase.
- Compare recent videos with older evergreen videos.
- Look for recurring beginner questions and repeated content demand.
- Record the strongest evidence against the niche as well as supporting evidence.

#### 2. Competition

Study approximately five relevant channels per shortlisted niche.

Record:

- Channel name and URL.
- Public subscriber count, if displayed.
- Channel creation date, if available.
- Recent upload frequency.
- Views and publication dates for selected recent videos.
- Whether smaller channels have videos that substantially outperform their own typical view counts.
- Whether search results are dominated by established brands.
- Gaps in existing videos that a new creator could address.

Define a **small-channel outlier** before counting one. For example, a video whose views are at least five times the channel's median views across its ten most recent comparable videos, where enough public data exists.

This is a screening heuristic, not a validated prediction of future success. Record missing data instead of guessing.

#### 3. Production feasibility

Write down a repeatable workflow for:

- Research.
- Scriptwriting.
- Voice recording or licensed synthetic narration.
- Visuals and screen recordings.
- Editing.
- Captions and thumbnail.
- Fact-checking and final review.

Estimate the time required, then verify the estimate with a production test on Day 5.

#### 4. Originality and usefulness

List ten potential videos that offer something distinctive, such as:

- An original test.
- A real comparison.
- A practical demonstration.
- A template or checklist.
- A transparent experiment.
- A carefully sourced explanation.
- A solution to a recurring audience problem.

Reject ideas that depend mainly on copying competitors' scripts, lightly rewriting existing articles, or assembling other people's clips.

#### 5. Monetization

List plausible monetization routes:

- YouTube advertising, if eligible.
- Affiliate links, where permitted.
- Original digital products.
- Sponsorships.
- Services related to the channel's subject.

Verify current YouTube Partner Program eligibility requirements using official YouTube Help documentation.

Check the terms, geographic availability, payment requirements, and YouTube rules for any affiliate program being considered.

Do not assume a new channel will earn money. Do not estimate revenue without defensible evidence.

#### 6. Copyright, licensing, and platform policy

Identify the planned use of footage, images, music, articles, diagrams, and software interfaces.

Classify each asset as:

- Created by the channel owner.
- Public domain, with status verified.
- Licensed for the intended use.
- Requiring permission.
- Uncertain and requiring further review.

Read current YouTube guidance on reused content, inauthentic content, and monetization.

A copyright-safe asset does not automatically make a video eligible for monetization. Originality, value, and platform policy compliance must be considered separately.

#### 7. Beginner suitability

Assess whether the creator can produce accurate, useful content without:

- Expensive equipment.
- Paid subscriptions.
- Professional credentials that the creator does not have.
- Continuous breaking-news coverage.
- Large quantities of licensed footage.
- Claims that cannot be independently verified.

### Phase D: Production Tests — Day 5

Choose up to three shortlisted niches.

For each niche, produce one private test video of approximately 60–90 seconds using free tools.

The test does not need to be published.

Record:

- Total production time.
- Research and fact-checking time.
- Script length.
- Tools used and their current free-tier limitations.
- Source URLs and asset licenses.
- Editing difficulties.
- Whether the result provides genuine value.
- What would need to improve before publication.

Do not use other creators' footage or music unless the intended use is properly authorized.

### Phase E: Score the Evidence — Day 6

Score only criteria supported by evidence.

Every score must link to at least one supporting source or documented production test.

Use `Insufficient evidence` when evidence is missing. Do not convert missing information into a score of 3.

Apply the rubric in Section 2 and record confidence separately.

### Phase F: Decision Review — Day 7

Compare the shortlisted niches.

Before deciding:

1. Review supporting and contradictory evidence.
2. Confirm that the proposed format can be produced consistently.
3. Review copyright and monetization risks.
4. Compare actual production-test times.
5. Check whether the evidence is strong enough to justify a decision.
6. Document the chosen niche, alternatives, risks, and reasons.
7. Record what future evidence would cause the decision to change.

If the leading scores are within 0.3 points, do not force a winner. Run another test or gather more evidence.

The outcome may be `No decision yet` if the research is insufficient.

## 2. Scoring Rubric

Score each criterion from 1 to 5 only after evidence has been collected.

### Criterion Definitions

| Criterion | Weight | Score 1 | Score 3 | Score 5 |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Audience demand | 20% | Weak or inconsistent evidence of audience interest | Several relevant signals suggest sustained interest | Multiple independent signals support sustained or growing interest |
| Competition opportunity | 15% | Strong competitors dominate and no credible content gap is identified | Some gaps exist, but competition is substantial | Multiple credible opportunities for a differentiated new channel are identified |
| Production feasibility | 20% | Requires unaffordable tools or an unsustainable workflow | Feasible with meaningful time or skill constraints | Repeatable workflow demonstrated with free tools and manageable effort |
| Originality and usefulness | 15% | Mostly dependent on copying or rephrasing existing content | Some original angles are possible | Many repeatable formats provide demonstrable original value |
| Monetization potential | 10% | No realistic route has been verified | At least one plausible route has been identified | Multiple plausible routes have been verified as relevant and accessible |
| Copyright and reuse safety | 10% | Format depends heavily on uncertain or unauthorized assets | A mixture of safe and risky assets remains | The format can primarily use original or clearly authorized assets |
| Beginner suitability | 10% | Requires resources, expertise, or speed beyond current capabilities | Learnable with focused preparation | Accurate, useful content is feasible with current resources |

**Important:** These scores are evaluation criteria, not current assessments of any niche. No candidate has been scored yet.

### Weighted Score

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5.

Weighted total:

`Total = (Demand × 0.20) + (Competition × 0.15) + (Production × 0.20) + (Originality × 0.15) + (Monetization × 0.10) + (Copyright Safety × 0.10) + (Beginner Fit × 0.10)`

Maximum possible score: 5.0.

### Confidence Labels

- **High:** At least three independent, relevant sources support the assessment, with no major unresolved contradiction.
- **Medium:** Two relevant sources support the assessment, but significant uncertainty remains.
- **Low:** One source, indirect evidence, or an unverified hypothesis.
- **Insufficient evidence:** Not enough evidence to assign a defensible score.

Three sources are not automatically independent if they repeat the same underlying information.

### Decision Safeguards

- Do not score missing information as average.
- Any score of 1 for copyright safety or production feasibility triggers a review before the niche can advance.
- Do not advance a niche whose production method relies on assets with unresolved permission or licensing issues.
- A high weighted total does not override legal, safety, or feasibility concerns.
- Scores within 0.3 points require additional testing rather than an automatic winner.
- Record the evidence and reasoning behind every score.

### Evidence Required for Each Candidate

**1. AI tools and workflows**
- Verify the current free-tier limits of relevant tools.
- Check whether recent videos remain useful after product updates.
- Look for smaller channels with unusually successful videos.
- Test whether a workflow can be demonstrated independently.

**2. Free software tutorials**
- Search for beginner tutorials on specific free applications.
- Compare views, publication dates, and channel sizes.
- Identify questions that existing tutorials do not answer.
- Verify any affiliate program and its terms.

**3. Business case studies**
- Check whether company facts can be verified using reliable sources.
- Identify ten potential subjects with sufficient public documentation.
- Assess the work required to explain the business accurately.
- Check image, logo, and footage permissions.

**4. Science explainers**
- Identify trustworthy primary or secondary sources.
- Check whether simple diagrams can explain the topic.
- Verify the accuracy of claims before scripting.
- Investigate the feasibility of original demonstrations.

**5. Technology explainers**
- Compare evergreen topics with news-dependent topics.
- Identify gaps in existing explanations.
- Check whether the necessary visuals can be created or licensed.
- Test the creator's ability to explain the subject accurately.

**6. History**
- Identify reliable historical references.
- Check public-domain status and individual image licenses.
- Compare smaller channels with established channels.
- Determine whether narration and original diagrams can reduce costs.

**7. Unusual true stories**
- Verify claims with multiple reliable sources.
- Avoid unsupported allegations about living people.
- Consider the privacy and dignity of victims.
- Verify the rights to all photographs, clips, and other assets.

**8. Productivity and study skills**
- Identify repeated audience problems.
- Design experiments that test tools or methods rather than simply repeating advice.
- Compare existing formats and competition.
- Check whether the creator can demonstrate real results.

**9. Personal finance basics**
- Verify relevant official guidance and jurisdiction-specific rules.
- Avoid personalized financial recommendations.
- Assess the risk of misleading viewers.
- Do not advance this niche without a credible accuracy and review process.

**10. Geography and culture**
- Verify maps, images, and cultural claims.
- Check licenses for geographical data and visual assets.
- Compare production effort with audience interest.
- Avoid stereotypes and oversimplified cultural explanations.

**11. Mini-documentaries**
- Identify legal and affordable footage sources.
- Estimate the research and editing time.
- Review YouTube's current reused-content and monetization guidance.
- Test whether original visuals can replace expensive footage.

**12. Free-tool career and skill guides**
- Identify specific skills people seek to learn.
- Verify that the recommended resources are genuinely accessible.
- Test a learning roadmap before recommending it.
- Check whether the content can remain useful as tools change.

## 5. Seven-Day Validation Plan

**Estimated time:** Approximately 1.5–2.5 hours per day. This is a planning estimate, not a guaranteed completion time.

All work remains private. No publishing, spending, or unnecessary account creation.

### Day 1 — Setup and Initial Screening

- Create the folder structure in Section 6.
- Review all 12 candidate niches.
- Write one channel promise for each.
- Identify three possible beginner video topics per niche.
- Record obvious production, expertise, and copyright concerns.
- Select four preliminary candidates for deeper investigation.

Do not assign evidence-based scores yet.

### Day 2 — Initial Demand Research

- Research the first six candidates.
- Use three seed searches per niche.
- Record up to five relevant video results per search.
- Capture autocomplete suggestions and Google Trends observations.
- Save dates and source URLs.
- Mark all unverified interpretations as hypotheses.

### Day 3 — Complete Demand Research

- Repeat the same process for the remaining six candidates.
- Compare evidence using the same collection rules.
- Identify the four strongest candidates for further investigation.
- Record why the other candidates were not shortlisted.

A shortlist is provisional and must not be described as a proven ranking.

### Day 4 — Competition, Originality, and Risk

For the four shortlisted candidates:

- Examine approximately five relevant channels per niche.
- Record available subscriber counts, recent upload dates, and selected video views.
- Look for small-channel outliers using the defined heuristic.
- Identify audience questions that existing videos do not adequately answer.
- List ten possible original video ideas per shortlisted niche.
- Check the main copyright, licensing, and platform-policy risks.

If evidence is incomplete, record the gap rather than filling it with assumptions.

### Day 5 — Production Feasibility Tests

- Select up to three candidates.
- Create one private 60–90-second test video per candidate.
- Use free tools and original or properly licensed assets.
- Record the real time required for research, scripting, recording, editing, and review.
- Note what was difficult and what could be repeated efficiently.

Do not publish the test videos.

### Day 6 — Scoring and Comparison

- Complete the evidence-based scoring sheet.
- Link each score to its supporting sources or production test.
- Apply the weighted scoring formula.
- Add a confidence label to each criterion.
- Flag unresolved risks.
- Create a provisional ranking only if the evidence supports one.

### Day 7 — Decision Review

- Review the top candidates and contradictory evidence.
- Compare actual production time and originality.
- Confirm that the proposed workflow is affordable and repeatable.
- Check current monetization and licensing constraints.
- Choose a niche only if the evidence is adequate.
- Otherwise, record `No decision yet` and specify what needs further research.

Save a decision memo explaining the chosen niche, alternatives, supporting evidence, risks, and conditions that would change the decision.

### Evidence Logging Rules

- Record the date for every observation.
- Keep source URLs and the actual observations that support each claim.
- Clearly separate verified observations from hypotheses.
- Preserve contradictory evidence.
- Do not commit private personal information, passwords, or API keys.
- Use descriptive screenshot filenames such as `ai-tools_youtube-search_2026-10-30.png`.
- If a source changes or disappears, retain the date of the original observation and recheck before making a current claim.
